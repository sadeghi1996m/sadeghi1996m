I am **Mohammad Sadeghi**, a researcher in signal processing based in Grenoble, France. My work focuses on recovering useful information from noisy measurements, with applications to graph signals and brain–computer interfaces.

I recently defended my cotutelle PhD in Signal, Image, Speech and Telecommunications at **Université Grenoble Alpes / Grenoble INP** and **Sharif University of Technology**, supervised by **Bertrand Rivet** and **Massoud Babaie-Zadeh**. During my PhD, I worked in the [Vision & Brain Signal Processing (ViBS) team at GIPSA-lab](https://www.gipsa-lab.grenoble-inp.fr/equipe/vibs) and the Digital Signal Processing Laboratory at Sharif.

My thesis, *Optimal sensor placement for source separation with application to event-related potential estimation*, studied how to choose sensor locations that improve signal recovery under noise and uncertainty.

## Research interests

My research brings together **graph signal processing**, **blind source separation**, and **statistical modeling and optimization**. I have developed spectral methods for separating graph signals and methods for selecting informative sensor locations, including EEG electrodes for P300 brain–computer interfaces.

I am interested in extending this work toward **graph machine learning**, **geometric deep learning**, and **statistical inference on graphs**.
